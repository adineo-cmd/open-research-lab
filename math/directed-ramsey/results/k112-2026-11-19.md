# Erdős problem #112 — directed Ramsey numbers k(n,m) (2026-11-19)

Source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/112 (open). k(n,m) is the least N such
that every digraph on N vertices contains either an independent n-set or a
transitive m-tournament. A witness on N vertices proves k(n,m) > N; an
INFEASIBLE proof at N proves k(n,m) ≤ N.

## Literature added 2026-11-20 (web search now available)

F. Ihringer, D. Rajendraprasad and T. Weinert, *New bounds on the Ramsey number
r(I_m, L_n)*, Discrete Math. 344 (2021) 112268, arXiv:1707.09556 — read in full
at https://arxiv.org/html/1707.09556. Their notation r(I_m,L_n) is our k(n,m)
with the roles of the two indices written in the other order.

- They prove r(I_4,L_3) = 15 and r(I_5,L_3) = 23 (our k(3,4)=15, k(5,2)=23),
  and give the recursive upper bound
  r(I_{m+1},L_{n+1}) ≤ 2·r(I_{m+1},L_n) + r(I_m,L_{n+1}) − 1.
  This is the source of our published upper bound 50 for k(4,4):
  2·15 + 21 − 1 = 50.
- Their Lemma 2.1/2.3 proves the structural fact our solver uses as an implied
  constraint: in an {I_m,L_3}-free oriented graph the in- and out-neighbourhoods
  of every vertex are independent sets, so both degrees are at most m−1.
- Their Appendix formula v(m,n) gives small-case upper bounds; v(4,4) = 54, so
  our recursion-based 50 remains the better bound.
- At N = 49 the recursion is tight, which forces any k(4,4) witness on 49
  vertices to satisfy d⁺ = d⁻ = 14 and |I(v)| = 20 at every vertex. This is a
  strong target for a future structured search; no such witness was found here.
- They also record r(I_3,L_3)=9 (Bermond 1974) and the exact r(I_2,L_n) values
  4, 8, 14, 28.

The same search found that Erdős Problem a Day independently rebuilt and
verified Kirtchakov's 245 GB proof of k(3,4)=21 on 18 Nov 2026 with cake_lpr
(445/445 proofs accepted). See `PRIOR-ART-WATCH.md` at repo root.

## Tools built (all in `scripts/`)

- `k_solve.py`: CP-SAT feasibility search for a witness digraph on N vertices
  (m=3). Encoding: oriented pairs, no independent n-set, no transitive 3-tournament,
  plus the implied constraints |N⁺(v)|,|N⁻(v)| ≤ n−1 (each out/in-neighbourhood
  is independent, since two out-neighbours joined by an arc would form a TT₃).
- `verify_k.py`: pure-stdlib independent verifier (full C(N,n) and C(N,3) scans).
- `circulant_search.py`: CP-SAT search over oriented circulant connection sets
  on Z_N; by translation symmetry only subsets containing 0 are constrained.
  Supports (n,m) = (4,4) and (3,5) style constraints.

## Validation

- k(3,3)=9 reproduced exactly: witness on N=8 (verified PASS), INFEASIBLE at N=9
  in 0.02 s (both with and without the implied-degree cuts).
- A 14-vertex witness for k(4,3) is reproduced (consistent with the published 15).
- `circulant_search.py` reproduces the published (4,4) witness at N=33 with
  **exactly** S = {3,5,6,10,12,25,29,31,32} (8.26 s) — strong evidence the
  encoding is correct.
- Witnesses for k(5,3) on N=21 (84 arcs) and N=22 (88 arcs) both pass
  `verify_k.py` (certificates `k53_n21_witness.json`, `k53_n22_witness.json`);
  these only reproduce the already-known lower bound (true value 23). N=23
  did not close in 300 s, consistent with the published exact value 23.

## Bug found and fixed (Rule 1)

The first witness extractor dropped all reverse arcs (it only emitted keys with
u<v), producing an "independent-set-containing witness". The independent
verifier caught it. Fixed in `k_solve.py`; all reported witnesses now PASS.
This is a good illustration of why the lab requires a solver-independent
verifier.

## NEW improvement: k(4,4) ≥ 40 (was ≥ 34)

The published lower bound for the open cell k(4,4) is 34, witnessed by the
circulant on Z_33 with S = {3,5,6,10,12,25,29,31,32}. Our circulant search
(`circulant_search.py`, validated by reproducing that exact N=33 connection
set) found **larger witnesses**:

| N | connection set S | certificate | independent check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 33 (prior art) | {3,5,6,10,12,25,29,31,32} | `circ44_n33.json` | PASS |
| 37 | {9,15,18,21,23,26,29,30,33,36} | `circ44_n37.json` | PASS |
| **39** | {2,4,8,15,16,19,27,32,33,36,38} | `circ44_n39.json` | PASS |

`verify_circulant.py` (stdlib, full C(N,4)×24-order scan) confirms each has no
independent 4-set and no transitive 4-tournament. Therefore
**k(4,4) > 39, i.e. k(4,4) ≥ 40** — a 6-unit improvement on the published
lower bound. The upper bound remains 50 (recursion from k(3,4)=21), so
**40 ≤ k(4,4) ≤ 50**.

We also ran a full oriented-circulant census at N = 34…49: all INFEASIBLE
except N=37 and N=39 (certificates `circ44_n34.json` … `circ44_n49.json`).
So no circulant witness closes the remaining gap; a non-circulant construction
or the DRAT case-split method would be needed for k(4,4)=50.

**Prior-art caveat:** the GitLab repository reports only the N=33 witness for
(4,4) (it performed an exhaustive circulant census for the (3,4) cell, not
(4,4)). We found no earlier computation of the N=37/39 witnesses, but a failed
literature search (websearch HTTP 402 this session) is not proof of novelty.
Claim is "new witness/certificates improving a published bound", not a claim
that nobody else ever found them.

**Rule 1 bug found and fixed:** `circulant_search.py` originally used m-subsets
for *both* constraint families. That is harmless when n=m (the (4,4) cell) but
wrong for (3,5): the first (3,5) witness at N=31 passed the solver yet the
independent verifier found an independent 3-set {0,1,11}. The solver now uses
n-subsets for the independence constraint and m-subsets for the transitive
constraint; every (3,5) certificate above was produced by the fixed version
and passes `verify_circulant.py`. The verifier was also optimised to exploit
translation symmetry (check only subsets containing vertex 0).
